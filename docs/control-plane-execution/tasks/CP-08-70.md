# CP-08-70 — Idempotent job dispatch over the worker transport

- **Package:** 072 — Out-of-process plugin workers (PR 4)
- **Status:** in review
- **Depends on:** CP-08-67 (#815), CP-08-68 (#816), CP-08-69 (#817)

## Outcome

A plugin with the `Jobs` capability can now be handed background jobs:
`WorkerFrame` grows the job pair (`job_request` out, `job_result` back,
same one-JSON-line wire), and `SupervisedWorker::dispatch_job` /
`await_job_result` / `job_state` are the host's half of the contract.

The host's guarantee is **at-most-once dispatch**: a `job_id` is sent to
the worker once, ever — across crashes, restarts, and re-dispatch calls.
The `JobDispatchLedger` records dispatch intent *before* sending (a
crash between record and send cannot produce a second send), refuses a
known id outright, and records results as they arrive, including
unsolicited ones (worker evidence is never discarded).

## Rationale

- **Three honest dispatch outcomes, not a boolean.** `Sent` (recorded
  and delivered to a live worker), `AlreadyKnown` (the id is burned —
  nothing was sent, nothing will be), `WorkerDown` (no live worker:
  nothing recorded, so a later dispatch is safe — recording here would
  burn ids on jobs that never had a chance to run).
- **Ambiguous delivery stays visible.** A send that fails after
  recording leaves the job `Dispatched` forever: it may or may not have
  reached the worker, so re-sending could double-execute. "Never
  completed, visibly" beats "silently retried".
- **Execution-level dedup is the plugin's obligation.** The frame
  carries the `job_id` to the worker; a well-behaved plugin can dedup on
  it. The host enforces its own half (never sends twice) and no more —
  what happens inside the worker is the plugin contract, documented,
  not host-enforced.
- **Results and probes share one wire without losing either.** Both
  frame kinds arrive unsolicited, so every receive routes what it got:
  a probe stashes job results that arrive mid-wait, a job wait ignores
  health answers. The stash means a result landing during a probe is
  delivered to its awaiter, not consumed.
- **Capability before idempotency.** Dispatch refuses a manifest
  without `Jobs` (`WorkerError::CapabilityMissing`) before touching the
  ledger — an undeclared capability never burns a job id.

## Real-process testing

The re-exec trick grows a `jobs` mode: a full-protocol worker that
counts its received requests and reports the count on demand
(`{"report_requests": true}`). That counter is the proof of the core
guarantee: dispatch a job, complete it, re-dispatch (refused), then ask
the worker how many requests it saw — two, not three. Also covered: a
job round-tripping over real stdio; a completed job answering from the
ledger instantly; refusal without the capability; dead-worker dispatch
recording nothing; and a result arriving during a probe surviving it.

## Scope

- `plugin_worker_jobs.rs` (new): `JobRequest`, `JobResult`, `JobState`,
  `JobDispatch`, `JobDispatchLedger`; 3 tests.
- `plugin_worker_transport.rs`: job frame variants (wire shapes pinned),
  reader forwards all frames, public `send`, deadline-based
  `probe`/`await_result` with result stash.
- `plugin_worker.rs`: `WorkerError::CapabilityMissing`.
- `plugin_worker_launcher.rs`: `WorkerProcess::send_job`/
  `await_job_result`; `SupervisedWorker` ledger + `dispatch_job`/
  `await_job_result`/`job_state`; `jobs` child mode; 5 new tests.
- `lib.rs` re-exports.

The ledger is in-memory by design for now: it survives worker restarts
(the host does not restart with them) and is the seam a durable store
attaches to when jobs join the work graph. Remaining 072 gate items:
webhooks and scoped secrets.
